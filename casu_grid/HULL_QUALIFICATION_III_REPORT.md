## 2. Protocol

### 2.1 CasU recordings (task A)
* Runtime: the existing staged, isolated CasU runtime (`/var/tmp/reinforcement-unknown-cb1-runtime-mission12-20261012`), the existing
  instrumented harness (`PPO_Harness/RunCounterfactual.sh`, smoke mode: per-tick pre-FixedUpdate / post-FixedUpdate / post-Physics2D traces and the
  `Body.Ragdoll` call log), launched through its own launcher and owned-process cleanup. The harness sources in the historical checkout were not
  modified: a generated copy of the runner (two lines changed: project root, client path) lives in the output folder; the only new component is the
  recording **client** `source_harness/CounterfactualSmokeRecord.py`, protocol-identical to `CounterfactualSmoke.py` but with a richer result
  (per-decision death, ragdoll time, consciousness, shock, pain, blood volume, crouch, speed, tile, block-health window summary).
  Every recording exited with status 0; no process was signalled by this campaign; afterwards no game, Proton or client process remained.
* Spawn: benchmark spawn, grid (483, 1010) (`PPO_DIAG_PLAYER_FIXTURE_GRID_X/Y`), world seed 391728405, natural terrain (no fixture edits).
* **Tapes**: 10 random tapes, **seeds 2001–2010**, 200 decisions each (2,000 decisions, 20,000 physics ticks), generated by
  `validation/random_tapes.py` (`random.Random(seed)`): move_x uniform {0, 1, 2}, jump and crouch fair coins, neutral vertical 1, look 4/5, attack 0 (the
  marginal distribution of the earlier stress-test random tape). Because attack is never issued, no terrain damage can occur.
* **A/A repeats**: tapes 2001 and 2002 recorded twice. 2001 is bit-identical; 2002 differs by at most 2.9·10⁻⁵ m (first difference decision 179): the CasU noise floor.
* Time cost: 27 s per 200-decision tape.
* Command: `bash casu_grid/validation/record_casu_random_tapes.sh OUT 2001 … 2010` and `TAPE_SUFFIX=_aa … 2001 2002`.
* Packed (checksummed, `tools/pack_source_trace.py`) into `validation/casu_random_tapes/rand_<seed>/` — **not** into the historical fixture folder — with `outcomes.json`
  (per-decision outcomes and the ragdoll log). `tests/test_random_tape_validation.py` verifies checksums, row counts and that the tapes equal the generator output.

### 2.2 Matched replays (task B)
`isaac_player_ab.py --trace rand_<seed> --trace-root validation/casu_random_tapes --modes free,teacher,segment --player-collider {compound,convex_hull} --detail --step-clear 0.02`
(options identical to every accepted A/B run; the compound path reproduces the accepted `flat_master`/`ice_run` results bit for bit after these changes). 20 runs × 3 modes;
Isaac is deterministic, so there is one run per cell. *Teacher-forced* = each CasU tick seeds Isaac (no compounding), *decision-level* = resynchronised each decision,
*free-run* = tape from the first tick. Paired statistics with tape as the sampling unit: bootstrap 95 % interval of the mean paired difference, exact sign test.
Nothing is pooled across colliders; the protocol is the same for both.

### 2.3 Diagnostics outside the contract
* `max_depenetration_velocity` 10 and 30 m/s (default 100) as **DIAGNOSTIC ONLY** replays of the same tapes — used to attribute a cause, never adopted.
* `perf/collider_stress.py` (no CasU reference): 4 seeds × 64 worlds × 1,200 random decisions per collider (307,200 decisions each) to size the artefact.
* `validation/policy_transfer_check.py` (task D).

### 4.3 Isolated events versus systematic differences
* Systematic, in the hull's favour: ground velocity (−33 %), airborne wall contacts, ceiling position, wall contacts at the decision level — each with more ticks better than worse.
* Systematic, shared: ceiling and landing impacts (above).
* Isolated, collider-specific: the ejections of §5 (10 events in 3,550 hull ticks, 3 in 8,020 compound ticks; each followed by a different trajectory).

## 5. The outcome-distribution discrepancy (task C)

### 5.1 Hazards with CasU as reference
Exposure for Isaac is the number of decisions before the first termination (censored at 200); CasU has no terminations. Exact (Garwood) 95 % intervals.

| | decisions at risk | hard landings | rate per decision [95 % CI] | world-top exits | rate per decision [95 % CI] |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|---|
| **CasU** | 2,000 | **0** | ≤ 1.8·10⁻³ | **0 (capped at y = 512)** | — |
| compound | 793 | 1 | 1.3·10⁻³ [0.03, 7.0]·10⁻³ | 8 | 1.0·10⁻² [0.4, 2.0]·10⁻² |
| hull | 345 | **9** | **2.6·10⁻² [1.2, 5.0]·10⁻²** | 1 | 2.9·10⁻³ [0.07, 16]·10⁻³ |

First terminations (10 tapes): compound — 8 world top, 1 hard landing, 1 none; hull — 9 hard landings, 1 world top.

* The **hull's hard-landing hazard is ~14× CasU's upper bound** (6.5× at the lower end of its interval) and the intervals do not overlap. The compound's interval overlaps CasU's (a CasU zero in 2,000 decisions cannot exclude 1·10⁻³).
* **World-top exits are not a fidelity property of either collider**: CasU stops at the cap in 9 of 10 tapes while Isaac has no cap. The compound exits more often only because the hull
  usually terminates earlier through the artefact below.

### 5.2 Event attribution
* **Ejections.** Counting single-tick velocity drops below −30 m/s (never observed in CasU; its largest is −24.9): compound 3 in 8,020 ticks (0.37 per 1,000), hull 10 in 3,550 ticks (2.8 per 1,000), hull
  in 10 of 10 tapes. All 9 hull hard-landing terminations and the compound's one are preceded, within the three decisions before the landing, by such an ejection (a single-tick drop of 54–86 m/s, minimum vy −58 … −103 m/s), not by a long fall.
  Worked example (tape 2001, decision 8): the player is pressed under the spawn overhang; CasU's controller grows the collider (crouch 0.94 → 0.54) and Box2D pushes it down at 1–2 m/s per tick;
  the hull is pinned and reports vy −60 → −79 m/s for six ticks, then drops 2 m and lands "hard"; the compound is pinned with vy ≈ 0 and slides down slowly.
* **Larger sample without CasU** (stress test: 4 seeds × 64 worlds × 1,200 decisions per collider): decision boundaries with |v| > 40 m/s occur at **1.86** (compound) vs **8.55** (hull) per 1,000 decisions
  (571 vs 2,626 events; hull in all 64 worlds of every seed; compound in 54–58 of 64); hard-landing episode ends 1,130 vs 3,610; world-top exits 2,307 vs 1,114. CasU: 0 of 2,000 decision boundaries (≤ 1.8 per 1,000).
  The compound is therefore **not clean either**: its rate sits at the edge of what 2,000 CasU decisions can exclude.
* **Diagnostic (not adopted):** replaying the same tapes with `max_depenetration_velocity` 10 m/s halves the hull's hard-landing hazard (9 → 4 events; 2.6·10⁻² → 6.5·10⁻³ per decision; ejections 2.8 → 1.4 per 1,000 ticks)
  and does not remove it; 30 m/s leaves it unchanged (9 events). The compound's own numbers move in the other direction (ejections 0.37 → 0.95 per 1,000 ticks, hard landings 1 → 2 events, partly because longer exposure follows from fewer early terminations), so the cap is not a clean fix for either collider. In the single worked example 10 m/s removes the hull's −60 m/s ejection entirely. The CasU traces record `max_linear_correction = 0.2` m,
  `baumgarte_scale = 0.2`, 3 position iterations for Physics2D, whereas Isaac uses 100 m/s; the diagnostic therefore indicates the right *kind* of cause (penetration recovery after the collider
  grows into geometry) but not a validated fix. Tick-level fidelity ranking is unchanged in the diagnostic (hull better on 10/10 tapes).
* **Why not a wall-top story?** The earlier report suspected the wall-top corner behaviour of `course_v1`; these data show the population difference is ejections plus the missing top cap.

## 7. Uncertainty and the smallest useful follow-up
* Ten tapes establish the **tick-level preference for the hull** (10/10, p = 0.002) and the **absence of CasU hard landings** (upper bound 1.8·10⁻³ per decision), not the true long-run outcome distribution.
  The compound's hard-landing hazard (1.3·10⁻³) and its ejection rate cannot be separated from CasU's with 2,000 decisions; the hull's can.
* One spawn, one world seed, one policy family (uniform random incl. jumping and crouching), no attacking, so no terrain destruction or combat; the Isaac side has no top cap.
* CasU is deterministic to ~3·10⁻⁵ m on repeats, so differences reported here are far above the reference noise.
* **Smallest useful follow-up (needs separate authorization where it changes physics or the environment):**
  1. *No CasU recording needed.* Quantify the ejection process in isolation (stress run, ≥ 10⁵ decisions per variant; the metric and runner exist) for any candidate mitigation — e.g. a penetration-recovery limit motivated by the recorded
     Physics2D `max_linear_correction` — together with the 8 trajectory gates and the existing A/B traces.
  2. Add the missing world-top cap (CasU: solid at y = 512.0) so world-top exits stop being counted as outcomes.
  3. Re-run this exact experiment (the same 10 tapes, nothing re-recorded) and add ~30 new tapes (≈ 14 min of CasU time at 27 s per tape, ≈ 20 min of replay for both colliders). 40 tapes give a CasU hard-landing bound of
     ≈ 4.6·10⁻⁴ per decision.

## 9. Recommendation
**Retain the compound collider as the default and collect additional, targeted evidence before any promotion decision.**

* *For the hull:* clearly closer to CasU tick by tick on every aggregate (10/10 tapes), and it removes the compound's snags documented in the previous report.
* *Against promoting now:* on representative random play the hull ejects the player 4.6–7.6× as often as the compound (stress test / tapes) and its hard-landing hazard is ~14× CasU's bound, producing a hard-landing hazard (2.6 %/decision) that CasU
  does not show; because training episodes terminate on hard landings, promoting the hull would change episode statistics for any policy that jumps and crouches — an artefact, not CasU behaviour.
* *Not a reason to prefer either:* the world-top exits that made the compound look worse in the stress test are an environment gap.
* *Promotion criterion proposed:* hull ejection and hard-landing rates compatible with CasU (intervals overlap CasU's bound) with the tick-level advantage and all gates preserved; the follow-up of §7 is the shortest path.
